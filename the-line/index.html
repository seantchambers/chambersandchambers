Line

A line is a point that has started to move. It is the first thing a drawing does, and the first thing that happens on site.

Euclid defines a line as length without breadth. Kandinsky, in Point and Line to Plane, describes it as the track a point leaves when a force pushes it. The point was rest. The line is the first movement, and so the first tension, direction and speed.

On site a building begins the same way. Profiles go in at the corners, string lines are run between them, and the walls are set out to the string. A plumb bob hangs a vertical line. Before there is any material, there are lines in the air.

Drawings depend on lines doing different jobs. The Australian drawing standard, AS 1100, gives each type a role. Thick continuous lines show what you can see. Thin ones carry dimensions and hatching. Dashed lines show what is hidden, and chain lines mark centres. A good section reads before you read a word of it, because the line weights already say what is cut, what is beyond and what is only implied.

Taking a walk

Paul Klee taught the same idea at the Bauhaus. His Pedagogical Sketchbook (1925, the second Bauhaus book) starts with a point that sets off on a walk, freely and with no goal, and becomes a line. From there he builds everything else: lines that hold planes between them, lines that bend under weight, lines that circulate like water.

The line by hand, the line by instruction

Agnes Martin drew hers by hand. In paintings such as The Tree (1964), hundreds of graphite lines are ruled across a pale canvas into a grid. From across the room it looks mechanical. Up close every line wavers slightly, and the slight waver is the whole point.

Sol LeWitt went the other way. From 1968 his wall drawings were written as instructions, such as lines in four directions or lines that do not touch, and drawn by other people, often long after he wrote them. The idea was the work. Whoever held the pencil was carrying out a program. A plotter running a file does the same job, without the drift that gives a Martin its character.
